Note · Grounding

Missing evidence stays missing

The most useful thing an assistant can say is sometimes “the source doesn't say”. Build for that on purpose.

Ask an assistant something its documents don't cover and it will often fill the gap with something plausible. In manufacturing or life sciences, plausible and wrong is the expensive combination.

Two small tests

A made-up procedure: fill, mix, drain. The configuration defines fill and mix but not what ends the drain step. Ask “What ends the drain step?” The right answer: the document doesn't define it.

Two made-up lab notes: sample A under condition A, measured only at the start; sample B under condition B, measured at the start and the end. Ask which condition preserves the sample better. The right answer: the notes can't support that comparison.

Both are easy to get wrong in a way that reads well.

What makes abstaining possible

  • Keep the source reference with every extracted fact, so “where does this come from?” always has an answer.
  • Label facts, inferences and gaps differently in the data itself.
  • Evaluate faithfulness: is every claim in the answer supported?
  • Show gaps in the interface. A gap the user can see is a question they can ask a person.

This site works the same way: the box on the home page only answers from my notes and says so when it has nothing.
